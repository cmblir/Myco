# Myco mascot assets

The canonical character is the user's original [`../logo.png`](../logo.png).
Keep its charcoal-purple mushroom cap, lavender underside, white oval eyes,
rounded body, and spherical hands. New poses extend that identity; they do not
replace the logo with a newly designed mark.

The original `idle` and `planet` videos and posters remain available. `MascotClip`
owns clip playback, motion preferences, visibility, and still-image fallback.
The app, Dock, and browser icons retain their original artwork.

The notch uses five prepared state poses. Nearby text and progress indicators
remain the source of status information; the character is decorative.

Rebuild the five poses from the preserved green-screen PNG masters with
`python3 scripts/prepare-mascot-poses.py <source-directory>`, then rerun
`scripts/gen-notch-mascot.py` for the notch derivatives. Colour recovery uses
the original green-plate coverage before alpha cleanup; using cleaned alpha
for that calculation amplifies semi-transparent edges into a bright fringe.

| Pose | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `reading` | Ready to ask, read, or capture |
| `thinking` | A request, import, recording, or save is actually in progress |
| `success` | A completed import or capture |
| `attention` | Review, approval, missing evidence, or recovery is needed |
| `resting` | No active work, or a cancelled operation |

Use one representative character per surface. Small utility rows retain their
existing icons. Optional sprite motion settles once on entry and respects the
mascot setting, reduced motion, window visibility, and viewport visibility.

The notch (`NotchMascot`) layers its own eyes over the poses, so
[`notch/`](notch/) holds each pose with its eyes painted out (64 and 128 px),
the detached props of thinking and success as separate `-fx` layers, and
`anchors.json`, the eye and glow positions measured from the originals. All of
it is generated by `scripts/gen-notch-mascot.py`; rerun it after a pose changes.

The notch's clips live in [`motion/`](motion/) as pairs: `<name>.webp`, a
horizontal strip of square frames at 12 fps, and `<name>.json`
`{ "frames", "fps", "loop", "frameSize", "pad" }`. Each frame is the `notch/`
layers' crop (sheet 60,46 to 348,334) widened on every side, so hops and
falling props are not cut at the pose box. `pad` is that margin as a fraction
of the pose box. The keyer widens it by 45 px of the 384 px sheet (168 px
frames, `pad` 0.15625), which reaches the take's own top edge; clips keyed
before that use 36 px (160 px frames, `pad` 0.125). Either way the pose box is
128 px of a frame, as in the `notch/` layers. The player draws a frame
(1 + 2 × pad) times the pose box, centred on it, so the character keeps the
still pose's scale. A missing or malformed pair falls back to the pose without
a word. A clip replaces the pose layer, the drawn eyes and the cap glow while
it plays; it carries its own.

| Clip | From → to | Loop | Notch state | Source take |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `read-loop` | reading → reading | loop | running | `read-loop` |
| `think` | thinking → thinking | loop | saving | `think` |
| `shake-no` | attention → attention | once | rejected | `shake-no` |
| `doze` | resting → resting | loop | idle after 90 s of quiet | `doze` |
| `wake` | resting → attention | once | a peek from a doze | `wake` |
| `gulp` | attention → success | once | accepted | `gulp` retake (`absorb-v2` in the library) |
| `celebrate` | success → success | once | done | `celebrate` retake, its first 3.2 s |
| `look-around` | attention → attention | loop | none: a stop while myco roams the screen | the app clip `clips/look-around.webm` |
| `enter` | resting → resting | once | none: going back into the notch after a roam | not made yet (krea) |

`enter` is a slot waiting on its take. Until `enter.webp` and `enter.json`
land here, myco goes home with a procedural squash and shrink of 400 ms; once
they do, the roam plays the clip where myco lands under the wing, for the
clip's own length, and lifts it into the seat (shrinking to the wing's size)
over its last 280 ms. No code changes. So the take should squash or sink in
place and end standing on the resting pose; the strip test then holds it to
every rule above.

A one-shot holds its last frame, which is the pose its state shows next, so it
hands off to the still without a jump. `lib/notchMascot.ts` keeps this table
as `CLIP_LIBRARY`, and its test holds the shipped strips to it and to the
1.6 MB budget (about 0.94 MB today).

`look-around` has no take of its own at hand, so
[`scripts/mascot-strip-from-clip.py`](../../../scripts/mascot-strip-from-clip.py)
frames the shipped app clip instead: it decodes the clip's alpha, scales each
frame back to the 384 px sheet, frames it like the keyer (160 px frames) and
grades it onto the attention pose with the keyer's colour fit, since the app
clips are not graded.

Provenance: krea.ai, Seedance 2.5 image-to-video (4 s, 640 px, 1:1). Each
take starts and ends on one of the five committed poses on a solid green
plate. [`scripts/mascot-key-clip.py`](../../../scripts/mascot-key-clip.py)
keys every frame (plate sampled from the border, alpha from green excess,
edges despilled), grades it with one colour fit per take onto the start
pose's colours, and writes the strip and its manifest. A light prop that the
take's top edge cuts (an orb that drops in from above the picture) is held
back while it is cut: the frame before it arrived holds, and once the prop is
whole it fades in over three strip frames. The keyer leaves the cap and body
alone, rim light included, so a cut cap still fails the strip test, and the
strip test also fails any light prop that shows cut flat near the top, however
faded at the edge. Ungraded, the takes
were about a third more saturated and bluer than the sheet, so the colour
jumped at every hand-off; the strip test holds each strip's first and last
frame to its pose layer's mean colour (ΔE ≤ 2).

`gulp` and `celebrate` are retakes, as is `hello` (not shipped, below). The
first takes' hops left the
generated frame (`hello` strip frames 18–22, `celebrate` 13 frames over four
hops, `gulp` 35 and 39–40) and cut the top of the cap flat, about 5 CSS px for
`hello` at the 56 px panel size; most of that cut was in the krea.ai frame
itself, so no padding could restore it. The retakes keep the cap inside the
picture, and the strip test fails any frame with anything opaque on its edge:

- `celebrate` hops to the sheet's y 9. The 36 px frame began at y 10 and cut
  the cap's top row at both apexes (strip frames 5 and 13); the 45 px frame
  starts at the take's own edge and leaves the cap 4 px. Its take settles on
  success by 3.2 s but runs 4.1 s, and done folds after 4 s, so the keyer
  takes its first 77 frames (39 strip frames, 3.25 s).
- `gulp`'s cap stays 13 px clear, but its orb drops in from above the take's
  picture (strip frames 2–6), so the take itself cuts the orb's top. A fade
  over the frame's outer 12 px still left a bright half-disc with a straight
  top beside the notch for 0.3 s, and it dimmed the cap's rim light at
  `celebrate`'s apexes. The keyer now holds strip frame 1 through frame 6 (the
  character stands still while the orb drops) and fades the whole orb in over
  frames 7–9, as it sinks into the cap.

Not shipped either: `sigh`. It runs 4.1 s, and cancelled holds for 800 ms, so
the driver folded it at strip frame 9, mid-inhale with its eyes still open.
Cancelled keeps the closed-eyed resting still and the procedural sigh until a
take of about 0.8 s exists; the strip test holds every state's one-shot to the
time the driver holds that state.

Nor `hello`. Its retake waves without leaving the ground and keeps 18 px of
its frame clear, but a clip stands the drawn eyes down, so over the peek the
cursor moved nothing for its 4.1 s (8.2 s after `wake`), and the eyes snapped
to the pointer when it handed back. Drawn eyes cannot sit on it either: its
face turns about 13 px of the sheet and tilts as it waves. The peek keeps the
attention still, its procedural hello kick and eyes that follow the cursor
from the first frame (decision 3); a hello with its eyes painted out and an
eye track to move the drawn eyes with the face could ship later.

Nor `listen`. The first take listened resting with its eyes closed, so every
recording snapped from the attention still to it, and its own baked glow
pulsed on a 4 s cycle while the voice drove nothing on screen. The retake
listens on attention with its eyes open, but its underside glow is still
baked into the take, and only the drawn cap glow follows the voice level. By
the last review's decision recording keeps the attention still and that
voice-driven glow, so the retake is not shipped.

Generation prompts and source masters are kept in
[`output/mascot-renewal`](../../../../output/mascot-renewal/generation-prompts.json).

## App clips (`clips/`)

The app surfaces play these through `MascotClip`; the table is
`SURFACE_CLIPS` in [`../../lib/mascotClips.ts`](../../lib/mascotClips.ts).
Each clip ships as an hvc1 `.mov` (WKWebView), a VP9 `.webm` (Chromium) and a
`.poster.png`, frame 0, which is the pose the clip starts and ends on. The
poster is the still for reduced motion, a failed decode and a finished
one-shot. `absorb` is the exception: it goes from attention to success, so it
never holds; it hands off to `read-loop`, or to `celebrate`, which starts on
the same success pose.

The macOS encoder receives premultiplied RGB, matching VideoToolbox's default
alpha mode. Straight RGBA input adds a bright fringe in native playback at
the cap boundary. Both clip scripts premultiply colour without changing alpha
before HEVC encoding. The native round-trip regression is
`python3 -m pytest scripts/test_mascot_hevc_alpha.py` (macOS, FFmpeg and Xcode
command-line tools required). See the [edge verification record](../../../docs/research/2026-10-07-mascot-edge-verification.md)
for the shipped asset provenance and comparison limits.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `welcome` | once | First-run wizard step 1 (96), About hero (128) | 256 px |
| `not-found` | once | Ask abstain card (112), Spotlight abstain line (56) | 224 px |
| `worried` | once | Crash screen (112) | 224 px |
| `absorb` | once | Ingest hero: source received (96) | 192 px |
| `read-loop` | loop | Ingest hero: run in progress (96) | 192 px |
| `celebrate` | once | Ingest hero: run done (96), Study end card (96) | 192 px |
| `think` | loop | Ask: answer pending, on the galaxy's hub (96) | 192 px |
| `look-around` | loop | Ask: empty, before a first question (144) | 288 px |

The 24 files total 1.48 MB. `lib/mascotClips.test.ts` keeps the folder under
the 4 MB app-video budget, checks that only clips a surface uses ship, and
checks that each clip is encoded at twice its largest box. The square krea
frame keeps room for hops and props, so its character fills about 64% of the
box. idle's fills about 82%.

Provenance: krea.ai Seedance 2.5 image-to-video produced each clip at 4 s and
640×640. Its start and end frames are the five poses on a green plate
(`output/mascot-renewal/sources/*.png`, per the motion library spec).
`scripts/mascot-key-clip.py` keyed each frame to RGBA at 384 px. Then
`scripts/mascot-app-clips.py` encoded the app files from those frames:

- each clip at 2× its box;
- green spill above red moved to lavender (the keyer left absorb's orb halo,
  celebrate's sparkles and the cap rim teal);
- a fade over the top rows on the frames where absorb's orb drops in from
  above the frame, and only those: on the later hop it made the cap see-through.

`absorb` is the library's `absorb-v2`. `celebrate` is a second take
(`celebrate-retake` in the keyed dir): the first one's hops cut the cap flat
on the frame top. The script stops on any frame that runs off the frame. To
rebuild, run `python3 scripts/mascot-app-clips.py <keyed-clips-dir>` from
`app/`. Keep its size table in step with `SURFACE_CLIPS`.
